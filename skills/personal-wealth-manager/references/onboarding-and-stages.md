# Onboarding and stage routing

## Interview behavior

Use progressive disclosure. Ask no more than three questions in one message. After each answer:

1. update the working ledger;
2. tell the user only the three to five changed facts or uncertainties that affect the next decision;
3. label important estimates and inferences;
4. point out material contradictions or possible double counting;
5. ask the next one to three highest-value questions.

Accept approximate values and ranges. Use “unknown” rather than pressuring the user to search immediately. Request screenshots only for account reconciliation or product details that materially affect the decision.

Do not ask a user to repeat information already given. If a screenshot and narration conflict, show both values and ask which date or scope is correct.

## Minimum viable intake

### Round 1: the decision

- What do you want to change or decide now?
- Is this your own money and decision, or does someone else need to consent?
- What would a successful result look like in one year?

### Round 2: safety and cash flow

- Reliable monthly income, variable income, and current sleep income?
- Necessary monthly expenses, debt payments, and large payments due in the next 24 months?
- Immediately available cash, protected deposits, liabilities, and private loans receivable?

For each large payment, record minimum / expected / maximum amount, date range, probability, responsible payer, and intended funding source.

For private loans receivable, first obtain the outstanding amount by borrower label, evidence, agreed due date, repayment status, and whether the user relies on repayment for a known payment or investment. Do not treat expected repayment as available cash.

### Round 3: investments

- Which platforms and currencies hold investments?
- Current value by meaningful holding, available cash, and product lockup?
- Why was each large position bought: self-research, allocation need, recommendation, promotion, or habit?

### Round 4: behavior and limits

- Largest historical drawdown and actual emotional response?
- Maximum portfolio loss that would change sleep, relationships, or daily decisions?
- Which positions have written caps, invalidation rules, or planned review dates?

Only run later rounds when relevant.

## Decision pools

Assign money to a decision pool before calculating one risk limit:

- **individual authorized** — the user may decide within stated constraints;
- **joint authorized** — affected people agreed on permitted use and risk;
- **joint unresolved** — ownership or household relevance exists but consent is not established;
- **earmarked** — reserved for a named payment, buffer, dependant, or contractual duty.

Calculate liquidity, investable amount, and risk limits per pool. Show all-related-assets ratios only as context. Never use a large joint or earmarked balance to make a concentrated personal portfolio appear safer.

For a maturing deposit, separately record original principal, interest, expected actual receipt, decision pool, maturity date, renewal offers and terms, early-withdrawal consequences, proposed bank split, and current official deposit-protection rules that need verification.

## Stage model

Stages can overlap and are not age-based.

### Stage 0: stop negative compounding

Signals: overdue obligations, high-cost debt, recurring cash deficits, speculative leverage, forced selling, uncontrolled averaging.

Primary work: stop new damage, negotiate or repay expensive debt, protect essential life and work capacity, and remove leverage.

For consumer debt, obtain remaining principal, payment schedule, all remaining fees, disclosed annualized cost, early-settlement cost, whether fees are refunded, income stability, and the minimum cash needed to avoid reborrowing. Do not decide from an advertised monthly rate alone.

Use this order:

1. preserve a starter buffer so repayment does not immediately create new debt;
2. calculate the remaining all-in cost with and without early settlement;
3. protect contractual payments and essential life;
4. prioritize repayment when the avoided cost is high and the buffer remains intact;
5. if the cost or settlement rule is unclear, collect the bank contract or official statement before deciding.

### Stage 1: stabilize essential cash flow

Signals: little capital, unstable income, or one income source funds nearly all necessary expenses.

Primary work: record reliable versus variable income, protect essential payments, identify insurance or social-protection gaps that could force liquidation, and avoid committing money that is not genuine surplus.

### Stage 2: create surplus

Signals: income covers expenses inconsistently or money disappears without a record.

Primary work: separate necessary, discretionary, irregular, and productive spending; create a repeatable monthly surplus.

### Stage 3: create a buffer

Signals: no expensive debt but a small emergency can interrupt investment.

Primary work: liquid runway, sinking funds for known large payments, and no forced sale of volatile assets.

Set buffers in layers rather than applying a universal “three to six months” rule:

- **starter buffer** — next month's necessary expenses and contractual payments plus the user's most likely near-term disruption;
- **known-payment funds** — separately earmarked amounts for dated obligations;
- **full runway** — sized from income stability, dependants, medical/work risks, access to family support, and time needed to restore income.

State the assumptions behind the target.

### Stage 4: accumulate safe principal

Signals: positive surplus but low investment experience or low risk capacity.

Primary work: simple protected or low-volatility instruments appropriate to the user's current rules, plus investing education.

## Gate from repair to first financial investment

Do not treat this as a rigid universal formula. Require evidence that:

- no overdue or uncontrolled high-cost debt remains;
- a starter cash buffer exists and known near-term payments are funded;
- monthly surplus is positive and repeatable;
- the first investment will not use debt or needed cash;
- the user understands loss, liquidity, cost, and product role;
- the learning position has a small explicit cap and review rule.

If a gate is uncertain, use a reversible education or simulation step before real financial risk. Do not spend the starter buffer on investments or optional experiments.

### Stage 5: build the core

Signals: sufficient buffer and investable capital.

Primary work: a small, diversified, understood core portfolio with explicit allocation, contribution, and rebalancing rules.

### Stage 6: controlled exploration

Signals: core is stable; user wants sectors, individual securities, or active research.

Primary work: small research budget, written thesis, position cap, review cadence, and no contamination of the core.

### Stage 7: compound financial wealth

Signals: income-producing assets or investment income cover a meaningful share of expenses.

Primary work: improve income durability, reduce product, platform, counterparty, and concentration dependence, reinvest selectively, and preserve financial choice.

## Route selection

- Expensive debt or deficit always outranks new risky investment.
- A known payment inside the investment horizon outranks return seeking.
- Unreconciled accounts outrank product selection.
- A concentrated portfolio outranks discovering new ideas.
- Low income and low capital may mean that no financial investment is currently appropriate; route broader capability planning to `life-asset-planner`.
- If household consent is the binding constraint, present a transparent risk envelope and alternatives; never coach concealment.

## Clarifying “personal” versus “household”

Default ledger owner: the individual user.

Include household facts only to answer:

- How much of the money is actually available to this user?
- Which expenses or dependants could force liquidation?
- Which decisions require joint consent?
- Does the household have duplicated concentration or missing protection?

Do not require a spouse's full account inventory when it is unrelated to the current decision.
