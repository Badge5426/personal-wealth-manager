# Portfolio reconciliation and cleanup

## First build a position ledger

Use one row per meaningful position:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Account/platform | Where it is held |
| Product and code | Exact identity |
| Market/currency | Original denomination |
| Current value | Position value on a stated date |
| Cost basis | Platform basis and its definition |
| Realized/unrealized return | Keep separate |
| Inflows/outflows | Contributions, withdrawals, distributions |
| Liquidity | Trading, redemption, lockup, settlement |
| Fees/tax | Known or requiring verification |
| Underlying exposure | Region, asset class, sector, style, factor |
| Idea source | Self-research, recommendation, promotion, legacy |
| Current thesis | Why it should be owned now |
| Portfolio role | Core, defense, growth, income, research |
| Cap/invalidation | Maximum size and evidence to change view |

Group tiny remnants when their total is immaterial, but do not hide them if they indicate recurring clutter.

## Diagnose the investment-supermarket pattern

Treat a portfolio as an investment “supermarket” when several of these are present:

- many products have no distinct current role;
- tiny observation positions remain indefinitely;
- recommendations from managers, influencers, friends, or promotions accumulate without a portfolio-level limit;
- several products duplicate the same companies, sector, country, style, or factor;
- the user cannot state which holdings are core;
- position count exceeds the user's ability to monitor product changes, thesis validity, costs, and risk;
- old holdings survive only because selling would realize a loss or because nobody issued a new instruction.

Do not equate diversification with product count. Diagnose complexity by distinct underlying exposures, decision rules, monitoring burden, and whether each holding materially improves the target portfolio.

For cleanup:

1. freeze new additions to unidentified, overlapping, or role-less holdings;
2. reconcile all accounts and group economic duplicates;
3. define the target core, defensive, research, and liquidity buckets before deciding transactions;
4. retain a product only when its distinct role, evidence, cost, liquidity, cap, and review rule are defensible;
5. group tiny remnants into one cleanup queue unless fees, suspension, tax, settlement, or product rules require separate treatment;
6. migrate in stages when immediate exit would create avoidable cost, liquidity, tax, or execution risk;
7. record why each holding was kept, frozen, merged, trimmed, or marked as an exit candidate so clutter does not return.

The goal is not the fewest possible products. The goal is the smallest understandable set that still provides the required diversification, liquidity, and risk control.

## Screenshot reconciliation

When reading screenshots:

- note screenshot date and account scope;
- distinguish account total from position total;
- do not sum “all holdings” and “asset summary”;
- identify pending orders, frozen balances, and redeemable cash;
- recognize that cumulative return may include sold positions or distributions;
- recognize that displayed holding return may exclude historical realized gains;
- keep market currencies separate until an exchange rate and date are stated.

If a platform number cannot be reconciled, preserve it as “platform displayed” and show the unexplained gap.

## Exposure map

Map each holding to the underlying economic exposures, not only its product name:

- cash and deposits;
- domestic or foreign bonds and duration;
- broad equity;
- country/region;
- sector and theme;
- small/large cap or growth/value style;
- commodities or gold;
- foreign currency;
- property or infrastructure;
- private, structured, or opaque exposure.

Then detect:

- multiple funds owning the same major companies;
- the user's profession, employer, property, and portfolio all exposed to one economic cycle;
- duplicated sector bets across mainland, Hong Kong, and overseas accounts;
- a “diversified” list that is actually one factor;
- cash that is not genuinely available because it is earmarked.

## Holding review

For each material position ask:

1. What did you originally believe?
2. What do you believe now?
3. What evidence supports or weakens it?
4. What role does it play in the target portfolio?
5. Would you buy the same weight today with fresh cash?
6. What is the maximum acceptable position?
7. What evidence, date, or price-independent event triggers review?
8. Is a simpler or cheaper vehicle available?

Avoid decisions anchored to the purchase price.

## Minimum product-specific evidence

Use current official disclosures and record the date.

### ETF or index fund

- tracked index and construction method;
- largest underlying holdings and industry/region exposure;
- tracking difference, total cost, size, liquidity, and replication method;
- overlap with the user's other funds.

### Active fund

- current manager and tenure;
- stated strategy, benchmark, holdings, style consistency, turnover, and fees;
- whether historical return belongs to the current manager and process;
- overlap with cheaper or simpler alternatives.

### Individual stock

- business and economic return engine;
- balance-sheet and cash-flow quality;
- valuation date and assumptions;
- single-company, governance, and concentration risk.

### Bank wealth-management product

- whether it is legally a deposit;
- manager, underlying assets, net-value fluctuation, risk label, term, redemption, and fees;
- whether any apparent yield is historical, quoted, or guaranteed by contract.

### Insurance with cash value

- protection and investment portions;
- premium obligations, fees, surrender value by year, exclusions, and time horizon;
- consequence of reducing, borrowing against, or surrendering the policy.

If minimum evidence is missing, classify the position as observe/freeze rather than filling gaps with marketing text.

## Classification rules

### Core

- durable portfolio role;
- understood source of return;
- acceptable cost and liquidity;
- sized to survive realistic drawdowns;
- user can hold without constant prediction.

### Research

- small and capped;
- written thesis and invalidation condition;
- no need to average down;
- separate from household safety money;
- scheduled review.

### Observe/freeze

- insufficient product information;
- unresolved overlap;
- position too large to add;
- thesis may be valid but timing or migration needs care.

No new money until the named issue is resolved.

### Exit candidate

- no current thesis or role;
- unacceptable opacity, fee, or lockup;
- broken thesis;
- redundant complexity;
- risk exceeds target and cannot be justified;
- user would not buy it today and is waiting only for break-even.

“Exit candidate” is a decision-analysis label, not an instruction to sell immediately.

For financial investments, show two columns in one row: **asset-quality judgment** and **portfolio role**. Do not duplicate the same holding in a second quality-asset table.

## Build target before transactions

Create the target architecture first:

1. liquidity and near-term spending;
2. stable/defensive bucket;
3. diversified core growth bucket;
4. research or concentrated-risk budget;
5. when the sleep-income route applies, a budget and cap for owned income-producing financial assets.

Set maximum numbers of core and research positions if complexity is a known problem.

## Migration plan

For each current position choose:

- keep;
- stop adding;
- merge or simplify;
- trim in stages;
- exit when liquidity or product conditions permit;
- collect missing information first.

Check current official rules for fees, redemption, tax, foreign exchange, lockups, and settlement. Explain why the sequence reduces a specific risk.

Do not recommend “doing T” or frequent trading as a recovery method unless the user demonstrates a tested edge, explicit rules, cost accounting, and a strictly separate risk budget.

## Portfolio behavior rules

- Precommit caps before the next price move.
- Define each cap explicitly as current market exposure, cumulative gross contributions, net contributions after sales, or stress-loss amount. These measures are not interchangeable. When more than one cap applies, use the first binding limit.
- Do not treat a price decline as newly available contribution capacity. A market-value cap may fall while cumulative contributions and underwriting risk have already exceeded their limits.
- A lower price does not automatically increase the cap.
- Use a cooling period for new themes and large additions.
- Keep core contributions separate from research trades.
- Review by thesis and allocation, not by daily emotion.
- Rebalancing is a rule, not a prediction contest.
- Distinguish discipline repair from market timing. When a position exceeded a precommitted cap, size any corrective trim from the target architecture or the identifiable violating transaction; do not justify it only because the market rallied, fell, reached cost, or produced a convenient intraday price.
- A future maturity payment, sale proceeds, bonus, inheritance, or other expected capital does not retroactively authorize an existing cap breach. Recalculate limits only after the money is received, earmarked obligations and liquidity are protected, the decision pool is confirmed, and any required household consent is obtained.
- Never use leverage by default.
- Never conceal investment risk from a spouse or co-owner.
