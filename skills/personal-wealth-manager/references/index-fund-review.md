# Index fund and ETF review

Use this module only when an index fund or ETF is a material current holding, proposed core, replacement candidate, or source of concern. Do not load it for an ordinary cash-flow review.

## Contents

1. Establish exact identity and decision
2. Use evidence in order
3. Audit the index and product
4. Decompose return and test portfolio fit
5. Apply the incomplete-information stop and replacement evidence gate
6. Review disappointing performance
7. Produce the review

## Establish exact identity and decision

Record both the fund or ETF code and the tracked index code. Similar marketing names may refer to different rules.

State the decision before collecting comparisons:

- retain, add, freeze, reduce, or replace a current holding;
- choose a core, defensive, growth, income, or research role;
- investigate disappointing performance or a methodology change;
- compare two or more candidates for one defined role.

Do not compare products until the money pool, horizon, target role, maximum exposure, and binding risk limit are known.

## Use evidence in this order

1. current official index methodology and maintenance rules;
2. official index factsheet and constituent data on a stated date;
3. fund prospectus, product summary, periodic reports, and exchange disclosures;
4. audited or reproducible data for additional analysis;
5. commentary only as a hypothesis source.

Preserve the source date. Treat missing official evidence as a reason to observe/freeze, not as permission to use marketing claims.

## Audit the index rule

Review eight dimensions:

1. **Objective and universe** — market, share class, geography, size segment, and intended representation.
2. **Eligibility** — listing age, liquidity, free float, profitability, ESG, connectivity, foreign-ownership, or other screens.
3. **Selection** — ranking variable, fixed count or coverage target, committee discretion, buffers, fast entry, and deletion rules.
4. **Weighting** — free-float market cap, equal weight, factor score, dividend, volatility, caps, and concentration constraints.
5. **Maintenance** — review frequency, reference dates, turnover limits, corporate actions, suspensions, and methodology-change governance.
6. **Current exposure** — largest holdings, industries, regions, size, value/growth, quality, momentum, dividend, currency, and concentration.
7. **History and return definition** — launch date versus back-calculated history; price, total-return, and net-return versions; rolling periods rather than one favorable endpoint.
8. **Failure mode** — the conditions under which the rules can lag, concentrate, churn, become costly, or cease to serve the assigned role.

Do not describe a market-cap index as automatically buying high and selling low. Examine boundary reconstitution, buffers, turnover, and current concentration. Do not describe an industry-balanced, dividend, value, quality, equal-weight, or low-volatility index as neutral; these are rule-based tilts with distinct failure modes.

## Audit the investable product separately

Check:

- replication method and permitted derivatives or securities lending;
- assets under management, trading liquidity, spread, premium/discount, and creation/redemption conditions;
- management, custody, index-licensing, transaction, and platform costs;
- tracking difference and tracking error over comparable periods;
- distributions and whether reported returns assume reinvestment;
- share class, currency, tax, settlement, suspension, and closure or merger risk.

An attractive index can still have a poor implementation. A well-run fund cannot repair an unsuitable index role.

## Decompose return without false precision

Use scenarios rather than a promised annual return:

`approximate long-run total return = starting cash yield + per-share fundamental growth +/- valuation change - dilution - product drag - tax drag`

Do not substitute current ROE, GDP growth, dividend yield, or historical index CAGR for expected investor return. Explain how retained earnings, incremental returns on capital, buybacks, issuance, valuation, fees, and reinvestment affect the result. Show conservative, base, and favorable assumptions when the decision depends on expected return.

## Test portfolio role and overlap

Judge the index inside the target portfolio, not in isolation:

- identify what distinct exposure it adds;
- calculate or qualitatively map overlap in top holdings, industries, regions, size, and factors;
- distinguish diversification by economic exposure from a larger product count;
- stress the combined portfolio, including income and property exposures that share the same cycle;
- verify that an equity income or low-volatility index is not being treated as cash, a deposit, or capital protection.

A broad index may be suitable as an equity core while remaining unsuitable as the user's only wealth core. Multiple broad indices may still be one duplicated exposure.

## Stop when decision inputs are incomplete

Do not issue buy, hold, sell, add, reduce, replace, migration, or allocation-percentage instructions unless all of these are known well enough to support the decision:

- exact current product and tracked-index identity;
- current value, portfolio weight, cost and liquidity constraints;
- decision pool, ownership and required household consent;
- money horizon, near-term payments and protected liquidity;
- target role and target architecture;
- objective risk capacity, subjective tolerance and binding loss limit;
- comparable official evidence for every replacement candidate.

When any item is missing:

1. label the decision pending and withhold a transaction recommendation;
2. do not convert missing evidence into a hold, freeze, no-new-money, or zero-trade instruction;
3. ask at most three decision-critical questions;
4. provide only scenario arithmetic or an evidence checklist that does not depend on invented inputs.

Never invent a default 80/20, 60/40, 20%, 25%, staged-sale fraction, contribution amount, or other portfolio weight. “Conservative,” “small,” “partial,” and “staged” still require an explicit cap and a reason derived from the user's risk budget.

Do not manufacture timing rules such as “do nothing today,” “do not buy or sell before review,” “review in seven days,” or “wait for break-even.” State only that the available facts do not support a transaction conclusion. The user retains control of any action while the review is incomplete.

## Apply a replacement evidence gate

Before recommending replacement or a material role change, require all of the following:

1. name a structural reason involving role, methodology, exposure, cost, tracking, liquidity, or changed constraints;
2. compare candidates on the same dates, currency, and price/total/net return definition;
3. separate live history from back-calculated history and disclose a short live record;
4. test overlap and identify every new factor or concentration introduced;
5. calculate transaction, tax, spread, lockup, and tracking consequences;
6. define the target architecture and cap before sizing a migration;
7. state evidence that would favor retaining the current holding;
8. use staged migration when urgency is absent and execution risk is material.

Recent outperformance, a compelling narrative, a loss, or the desire to get back to cost is insufficient. Do not hard-code a preferred domestic or foreign index, and do not default to CSI 300, CSI A500, dividend low volatility, S&P 500, or any other named benchmark.

## Review disappointing performance

Separate four judgments:

- **index outcome** — return over the stated period and return definition;
- **product execution** — tracking, costs, distributions, and liquidity;
- **portfolio design** — role, sizing, diversification, horizon, and risk-budget fit;
- **decision process** — whether the user followed precommitted rules.

Calculate the user's money-weighted result when cash-flow dates are available. A negative rolling return is not by itself proof of a bad asset; a later recovery is not proof that the original size or role was correct.

Show break-even arithmetic only as education. Do not recommend unlimited averaging, leverage, frequent trading, or waiting for cost solely to manufacture recovery. If the position breaches the binding risk limit, repair the portfolio even when the position is below cost.

## Produce the review

Use one row per candidate:

| Index and product | Intended role | Rule and return engine | Main exposures | Live/backtest evidence | Product drag | Failure mode | Portfolio fit | Action conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Close with:

1. facts and unresolved evidence;
2. the strongest case for retaining the current holding;
3. the strongest case for changing it;
4. overlap and stress implications;
5. provisional classification: core, research, observe/freeze, or exit candidate;
6. one reversible next action and a price-independent review trigger.

Do not issue a transaction command when identity, official methodology, product implementation, target role, or risk budget remains unresolved.
